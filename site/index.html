    MCP

    Plug an agent in.

    An agent gets a seat: its own key, made on your machine, and the limits you sign — trade, move, pay, earn, each with a per-order line, a total and an expiry. 32 tools: read, trade, move, pay, earn, ask, wait, remember. Reads are never gated. Anything over a limit is refused before a card is raised.

    npm run portfolio:mcp
    claude mcp add portfolio -e PORTFOLIO_URL=http://127.0.0.1:4820 -- npx tsx src/portfolio/mcp.ts
    200Done. An order comes back (maybe still open); a movement comes back as a payment (maybe still on its way).
    202A card for the owner first.
    401This key is not a signer: never let in, expired, or revoked.
    409Refused, with a code that says which line it crossed.

    Security

    Who can move money.

    Owner

    A device key the browser cannot export. Every real-money action is one signature, with “What you sign” laid out first; the price or address is asked again before it runs, and if it changed, it does not.

    Agent

    Its own key, made on your machine, plus the limits you signed: trade, move, pay, earn — each with a per-order line, a total and an expiry.

    Venue

    Its own location rules, asked from your own network every 30 minutes and shown in its own words. Nothing here works around them.

    Guard: what agents ask for waits for you on a card. Beast: inside the limits you signed, it goes at once. Guard is one click; Beast is signed.

    What this does not prove

    • Real connections and real-money writes have only been tested against stand-ins: no real key and no real wallet has been used here. Connect with a read-only key first; to write, start with a small cap and small amounts.
    • What has really been read is keyless public data: exchange prices, Kalshi, Polymarket and Polymarket US gateways, pre-IPO prices, LI.FI quotes.
    • The local file system is the trust boundary: whoever can write the home can change the ledger and the key files; agent seats under the same system user are not walled off from each other.
    • Custody, licences and who pays when something goes wrong are not software, and are not here.

      FAQ

      What is Gravit, and what is the Account?

      Gravit is an AI workbench for investment research: markets, strategies and several research perspectives, with the evidence kept in context. The Account is its funding account for agents — your real exchange, broker, wallet and prediction-market accounts on one page, where agents trade, move money and pay inside limits you sign, and you watch, steer and approve.

      Is any of this real money?

      When you run it, yes: real orders and movements at the accounts you connect are on by default, each at most $100 (--live-cap), and --read-only turns them off. The code itself has only been exercised against stand-ins: no real key and no real wallet has been used in its development, and what it has really read is keyless public data.

      Does it work from where I am?

      Each venue is asked from your own network, at start and every 30 minutes, and answers in its own words: reachable, not served here, close-only (Polymarket for the US: connect, sell and cancel, but no new positions — Polymarket US is a separate venue with its own key), or a residency rule its terms state (shown, never enforced). Binance.US, OKX US and Polymarket US are listed beside their parents where they serve you. Nothing works around a venue's rule.

      Can an agent empty my account?

      Only what you signed: each limit has a per-order line, a total and an expiry, and anything over it is refused. In Guard every request waits on a card that expires unanswered after 30 minutes; in Beast it runs inside the limit at once. Money goes only to places shown to be yours, and you can revoke any agent at any time.

      Why is there no hosted version?

      One operator, one machine, one principal agent; nothing runs unattended. The service listens on 127.0.0.1 only, your keys stay in files on your machine, and the owner is a device key in your own browser. Run it locally, or install it as a background service on your Mac.
